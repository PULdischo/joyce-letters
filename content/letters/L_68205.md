> care of <!-- alt reading: "c/o" --> [Gioachino <!-- alt reading: "Gioacchino" --> Veneziani](/people/vene45/),
> [Murano](/places/G_ITA_VENI_01/),
> [Venice](/places/G_ITA_VENI/),
> ([Italy](/places/G_ITK/))[^1]

My dear [Mr Pound](/people/poun85/)

Many thanks for the copy of [<u>Poetry</u>](/works/W_POETRY/) with your interesting essay and verses[^2]. I shall be very glad indeed to receive a signed copy of your book of verses[^3]. May I remind you that you promised also to send me a photograph[^4]?

I received also the agreement from [Mr Pinker](/people/pink62/) [^5]. I have written to say that [Mr Grant Richards](/people/rich72/) has the right of refusal (book rights) of [my novel](/works/W_APORTRAIT/). Subject to that condition I agree to place the book in his hands[^6]. As for the dramatic rights of my [play](/works/W_EXILES/) I agree to hand over these to him immediately[^7]. I could also give him the control of any magazine or review publications. It was very kind of you to call on him and so I shall not trouble you further. If Mr Pinker accepts these conditions I shall be very glad to accept his proposal which is, as you say, very fair.

I am glad to know that you have recovered from your influenza. We had a shock of earthquake here[^8] some nights ago – I mean, where I live: and the weather has been all <u>scirocco</u> and rain during the winter. I am not molested in any way.

I saw some time ago the Italian version of [<u>Countess Cathleen</u>](/works/W_COUNTESSCATHLEEN/) – by [Mr Linati <!-- alt reading: "Luciani" -->](/people/lina78/), I think. I am sorry that the version made by my friend [Mr Nicolò Vidacovich](/people/vida75/) was not completed owing to some misunderstanding (of a literary kind)[^9] between him and [Mr Yeats](/people/yeat65/).

sincerely yours

[James Joyce](/people/joyc82/)

Easter Eve 1915

---

[^1]: Because the war interfered with mail between England and the Austro-Hungarian empire (which included Trieste), JJ, who lived at this time with his family at [4 via Donato Bramante in Trieste](/places/G_ITA_TRIE_11/), used a mailing address in Venice of Gioacchino Veneziani, the father-in-law of [Ettore Schmitz (Italo Svevo)](/people/schm61/). Italy remained neutral until 23 May 1915.

[^2]: This was probably Poetry, vol. 5, no. 6 (March 1915), containing several poems by Pound – including “Provincia deserta” and “The Coming of War: Actaeon” – as well as the second installment of his three-part essay, “The Renaissance,” an assessment of American poetry and culture and a program for endowing and advancing the arts.

[^3]: Around this time, Pound sent JJ a copy of his slim volume [Cathay](/works/W_CATHAY/), published by [Elkin Mathews](/people/math51/) in London on 6 April 1915, and containing Pound’s sensitive renderings of Chinese poems from material in unpublished notebooks of the American Orientalist, Ernest Fenollosa (1853-1908).

[^4]: In his letter to JJ of ca. June 1915, Pound enclosed photographs of himself, including one of a portrait painted in Paris in 1911 by the American artist Eugene Paul Ullman (1877-1953), and another of a sculpture of Pound’s head (“hieratic, phallic, even,” as Pound told JJ) produced in London in 1914 by the French artist, Henri Gaudier-Brzeska (1891-1915). Pound promised to try to send JJ a “new photo” of himself “in good time” (Pound/Joyce 35). He eventually sent JJ “a job lot” of photos of himself on 23 October 1915 (Pound/Joyce 58).

[^5]: Pound enclosed with his letter to JJ of ca. 29 March 1915 a proposed agreement from the British literary agent James Brand Pinker (prob. 1862 or 1863-1922) to act for JJ. Pinker had written JJ on 10 February 1915, offering at [H.G. Wells](/people/well66/)’s suggestion to represent him (Ellmann, JJ 384). At JJ’s request, Pound met with Pinker in London and liked him, impressed by his desire to represent authors “who take their work seriously” (Pound/Joyce 34). Pinker until his death in 1922 represented JJ, having “little luck in marketing [JJ’s] peculiar merchandise” but serving as “the amiable recipient of the innumerable letters about small matters which Joyce soon began painstakingly to send him” (Ellmann, JJ 384). Pinker represented numerous modernist authors at various points, including Henry James, Joseph Conrad, [Ford Madox Ford](/people/ford73/), [T.S. Eliot](/people/elio88/), D.H. Lawrence, Katherine Mansfield, and Dorothy Richardson. Pinker’s sons continued to act for JJ after their father’s death (see [JJ to Pound, 20 August 1931](/letters/L_70011/), notes).

[^6]: JJ’s contract for Dubliners gave his London publisher Grant Richards (1872-1948) a right of first refusal of JJ’s work for several years, but in May 1915 Richards declined A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man – already being serialized in the Egoist in London – as did several other publishers, before B.W. Huebsch of New York published it in December 1916. The novel was first published as a book in England in February 1917 by the Egoist Ltd. of London, from American sheets because English printers worried about potentially scandalous content.

[^7]: JJ’s Exiles: A Play in Three Acts, which he wrote in 1914-1915, was published by Grant Richards in London in May 1918 and simultaneously by [B.W. Huebsch](/people/hueb76/) in New York. Richards signed the contract for Exiles in August 1917 after J.B. Pinker reluctantly gave him the first refusal of the play, as required by JJ’s Dubliners contract.

[^8]: A massive earthquake in January 1915 had caused thousands of fatalities in southern and central Italy, destroying the town of Avezzano, east of Rome. Slighter earthquakes continued in March and April, forcing many Italians to camp in the open air. The _scirocco_ or _sirocco_ is a hot, dust-filled wind from the Libyan deserts that affects countries and islands of the Mediterranean, including Italy, Sicily, and Malta.

[^9]: The verse drama Countess Cathleen by W.B. Yeats (1865-1939) was first published in 1892 and performed in Dublin in 1899. JJ, who attended the first performance while an undergraduate, refused to sign a letter of protest against the play. The play underwent much revision and was performed by the Abbey Theatre in Dublin in 1911 and published in 1912. By “Mr Luciani,” JJ means Carlo Linati (1878-1949), whose Italian prose translation of The Countess Cathleen was published with other Yeats plays in Tragedie Irlandesi di William Butler Yeats (Milan: Studio Editoriale Lombardo, 1914). It was not until 1918 that JJ began a correspondence with Linati, who later published Italian translations of [Exiles](/works/W_EXILES/), “Araby,” a portion of [Ulysses](/works/W_ULYSSES/), and Stephen Hero. According to JJ, he and his Trieste friend and pupil, the lawyer Nicolò Vidacovich, produced an Italian translation of The Countess Cathleen in 1911-1912, “[not] for the stage but for the pages of a review,” as JJ explained to Yeats in September 1912 (LI 71). JJ told [Harriet Shaw Weaver](/people/weav76/) in November 1916 that “the project failed as we had translated the first version and Mr Yeats did not wish that version to be offered to the Italian public” (LI 99). With Vidacovich, JJ also translated J.M. Synge’s Riders to the Sea in 1908-1909, but Synge’s heirs refused to allow an Italian performance. The translation was published some twenty years later as La Cavalcata al Mare in the Florentine journal Solaria, vol. 4, nos. 9-10 (September-October 1929): 3-16.

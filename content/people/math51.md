British publisher. Mathews and partner John Lane founded the Bodley Head press in 1887. His disagreement with Lane over the press’s publication of the risqué Yellow Book magazine led him to form his own firm in 1894. Mathews became one of London’s leading poetry publishers, issuing titles by [Yeats](/people/yeat65/), [Aldington](/people/aldi92/), and Masefield; he was also a supporter of the Irish Literary Revival, publishing [Synge](/people/syng71/)’s Riders to the Sea. [Arthur Symons](/people/symo65/) brought JJ’s collection of verse, Chamber Music, to Mathews’s attention; he published the book in 1907. Within months of the book’s appearance, JJ tried to interest Mathews in Dubliners, but was refused. In 1913 he again offered Dubliners to Mathews, and was again refused, although Mathews did eventually reissue Chamber Music in 1918. Mathews was [Ezra Pound](/people/poun85/)’s most important early publisher; until Mathews’s death in 1921 and the sale of his firm, he published some half dozen of the poet’s books. **William Brockman**

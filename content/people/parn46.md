Charismatic Irish nationalist politician, sometimes known as “the Chief” and as “the uncrowned king of Ireland”. He served as a Member of Parliament from 1875-1891 and was Leader of the Home Rule League from 1880-1882 and of the Irish Parliamentary Party from 1882-1891. His political heft and popularity declined sharply following the disclosure in 1889 of his affair with the married Katherine (“Kitty”) O’Shea. Parnell’s legacy pervaded Joyce’s works, notably in “Ivy Day in the Committee Room,” in the Christmas dinner scene in A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man (in which the support for and condemnation of Parnell are vividly portrayed), in the “Hades” episode in Ulysses, and throughout Finnegans Wake. **William Brockman**

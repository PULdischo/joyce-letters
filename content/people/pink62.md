Prominent British literary agent. After working as a correspondent in Turkey for the Levant Herald, he returned to England where he spent several years in editorial positions with Black & White and Pearson’s Magazine. Pinker established his agency in 1896 at a time when the profession of literary agent was a new one; Pinker, along with A. P. Watt and the American Albert Curtis Brown, was one of its forerunners. His early clients included Arnold Bennett, Joseph Conrad, Ford Madox Ford, D. H. Lawrence, Violet Hunt, and [H. G. Wells](/people/well66/), and he prided himself on promoting young emerging writers. It was Wells who drew Pinker’s attention to The Egoist’s serial publication of [A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man](/works/W_APORTRAIT/), which led to Pinker’s offering his services to JJ in 1915. From then through the end of the decade JJ and Pinker were in frequent contact, with JJ sending sometimes imperious and contradictory instructions, and Pinker replying with patience. He negotiated methodically with the erratic publisher [Grant Richards](/people/rich72/) for Joyce’s royalties, and was instrumental in placing [Exiles](/works/W_EXILES/) with Richards and in negotiating the contract for [A Portrait of the Artist](/works/W_APORTRAIT/) with [B. W. Huebsch](/people/hueb76/). Pinker’s negotiations with Huebsch for publication of [Ulysses](/works/W_ULYSSES/) marked the diminishing of JJ’s correspondence with him as publication plans were then made directly with Shakespeare and Company. On 8 February 1922, only days after the publication in Paris of [Ulysses](/works/W_ULYSSES/), Pinker died suddenly of influenza and pneumonia while on a trip to New York. His sons, [Eric](/people/pink91/) and [James Ralph](/people/pink00/), carried on the business of the literary agency. **William Brockman**

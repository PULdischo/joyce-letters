The son of a British father and Indian mother, Gilbert served in various civil service positions in Burma until moving in 1927 to Paris, the home of his wife, Moune. He soon became involved in the French translation of Ulysses, which he had first read in Burma. He and his wife became close friends with JJ and [Nora](/people/barn84/), the four traveling together to Salzburg and other vacation destinations. Gilbert was one of the most vigorous proponents of JJ’s work, publishing a number of articles on Ulysses and the developing “Work in Progress” beginning in 1928. Gilbert’s James Joyce’s Ulysses: A Study (London: Faber & Faber, 1930) (in which JJ himself had significant influence) was published at a time when its very subject remained largely unavailable in Great Britain and the United States; it continues to be one of the key secondary works on JJ’s book. Gilbert’s commitment to JJ’s legacy continued long after his friend’s death, with his editing of Letters of James Joyce (London: Faber & Faber, 1957). Apart from JJ, Gilbert was a prolific translator into English of contemporary French literature, notably works by Saint-Exupéry, Sartre, Cocteau, Simenon, Camus, and Malraux. The posthumous publication of Reflections on James Joyce: Stuart Gilbert's Paris Journal (ed. Thomas F. Staley and Randolph Lewis, Austin: University of Texas Press, 1993) made available a more unguarded and critical perspective on Joyce than in Gilbert’s earlier writings. **William Brockman**
